## TEMPLATE 1: The Before & After

### Template Structure (Verbatim)

**Opening:** TRANSFORMATION CONTRAST HOOK

**Middle:** VIVID PAIN STATE + TRANSFORMATION MOMENT + SPECIFIC PROOF

**Close:** BOLD PROMISE + TAP/CLICK CTA

### Template Breakdown (9-Step Process)

1. Opens with a TRANSFORMATION CONTRAST HOOK that immediately showcases before/after change
2. Vividly describes the painful "before" state that resonates with the audience
3. Introduces a dramatic TRANSFORMATION MOMENT that changed everything
4. Provides specific, compelling PROOF of the transformation's impact
5. Creates contrast between the prospect's current reality and the possible future
6. Explains just enough about the mechanism to create credibility
7. Emphasizes ease of implementation and rapid results
8. Includes social proof that reinforces the transformation's attainability
9. Closes with a Facebook-appropriate "Tap below" CTA that reinforces the transformation

### Key Principles

- Start with a dramatic transformation contrast that directly ties to the template concept
- Use specific timeframes in the opening to make the transformation feel concrete and rapid
- Create vivid contrast between the "before" and "after" states
- Include unexpected elements that challenge conventional wisdom
- Make the transformation feel both significant and attainable
- Focus on the emotional benefits beyond the practical outcomes
- End with a "Tap below" call to action that reinforces the core transformation benefit

## Glossary & Key Terms

**Copy Blocks:** Modular pieces of copy (hooks, pain states, proofs, CTAs) that can be combined using proven frameworks to create high-converting ads.

**Framework:** A proven structural template that sequences psychological elements in a specific order to maximize conversion (e.g., The Before & After, The Insider Reveal).

**Hook:** An opening line designed to stop scrolling and create curiosity (e.g., "Who Else Wants [blank]?", "The Secret of [blank]").

**Pattern Interrupt:** A statement or question that breaks through cognitive patterns and creates cognitive dissonance, forcing attention.

**CTA (Call To Action):** The closing instruction that prompts the prospect to take the desired action (e.g., "Tap below to...", "Click below to...").

**Psychological Trigger:** A specific neurological pattern that activates automatic responses (e.g., curiosity, loss aversion, status anxiety).

**Transformation Contrast:** The stark before/after comparison that makes change feel significant and attainable.

**Social Proof:** Real examples of people achieving results using the solution, typically quantified (e.g., "Over 8,000 high-performers...").

**Mechanism:** The explanation of how/why the solution works, provided just enough to create credibility without giving everything away.

**Identity Shift:** A change in how someone sees themselves, which then drives automatic behavior changes.

**Bottleneck:** A single hidden obstacle that, once removed, unlocks dramatic transformation.

**Overlooked Factor:** A crucial missing element that explains why conventional approaches fail.

**Quick Win:** A fast, tangible result that builds momentum toward larger transformations.

**Resource Constraint:** A limited resource (time, money, energy) that the prospect struggles with daily.

**Regret Minimizer:** Activation of anticipated future regret to create urgency in present decision-making.
