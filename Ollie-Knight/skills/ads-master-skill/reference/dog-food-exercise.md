# Dog Food Exercise + Hook-Body-CTA Cohesion + Andrew's Sober Living Worked Example

_Source: File 3 — Ad Diversification Mastery SOP. This is the diversification engine: list every reason someone would buy your offer, each becomes a hook, film hook+body+CTA as ONE cohesive unit per reason. Don't interchange components anymore (Andromeda kills surface-level variations). Andrew's Sober Living Homes is the proof point: 15 unique creatives, K spend, stable -50 CPL._

## Section: The Dog Food Exercise

### Purpose
Finding your diverse messaging angles for a product/service.

### Method (Step-by-Step)

1. **Identify the product**: Example uses "dog food"
2. **List diverse reasons people buy it**: Write down "a fuckload of reasons that somebody would buy" this product
3. **Each reason becomes a hook**: These become the things you can start an ad with and center the ad around

### Verbatim Example List: Dog Food Reasons

- Healthier ingredients
- Joint health for older dogs
- Live longer/healthier life
- Gut balance and digestion
- Tastier (doesn't smell like shit)
- No dog farts (seriously)
- Less hair shedding
- More energy for the dog
- Couch doesn't get messy
- Not highly processed
- Fewer vet visits
- Better coat and skin

## Section: The Hook-Body-CTA Framework That Actually Works

### The Old Approach (No Longer Effective)

**Historical method**: Components were interchangeable
- You could keep body + CTA the same
- Only rotate the hook
- Film body/CTA once, then film 10-15 different hooks separately
- Edit them together later

**Current effectiveness**: Still has some effectiveness IF you can produce 50+ creatives per week, BUT exposes you to significant volatility at higher spend levels.

### The New Approach (Recommended)

**Film the entire ad as one cohesive unit** aligned around one specific reason someone would want your offer.

**Key Principle**: "Your hook, body, and CTA need to be completely aligned around one specific reason someone would be interested in your offer. You can't just swap hooks onto the same body copy anymore."

**Do Not**: Interchange components.  
**Do**: Film whole thing as one cohesive unit about that one reason.

## Section: Real Example - Andrew's Sober Living Homes

### The Results (July 2025, During Andromeda Rollout)

- **Cost Consistency**: Costs didn't fluctuate once all month
- **CPL Range**: Maintained $40-50 cost per qualified call
- **Spend**: Approximately $40,000 for the month
- **ROAS**: Excellent
- **Lead Quality**: Consistent
- **Cost Per Call**: Consistent with no volatility

### Critical Success Factor

"Andrew created fifteen completely unique creatives, each built around a different reason someone would be interested in sober living homes. Each ad was filmed from start to finish with the hook, body, and CTA all aligned around one specific angle."

### Two Example Ads (Verbatim Breakdown)

#### Ad 1 - Business Opportunity Angle

**Hook**: "You ever heard of 9 people paying rent on one single family home legally and having the government help you fill it?"

**Body Focus**: 
- Business model framing (not Airbnb, not Section 8)
- Regular house conversion
- 2-3 people per bedroom
- Clean and sober residents
- Rent paid through agencies/disability/social security
- Government support for placement

**Psychological Trigger**: Appeals to people interested in business opportunity and financial model.

#### Ad 2 - Social Problem Angle

**Hook**: "Let's be real. America has a drug problem. But what happens after someone gets sober?"

**Body Focus**:
- Social issue framing
- Rehab ends, insurance stops
- People have nowhere to go, no family or housing
- Just hope
- Sober living as the solution for people walking the same path

**Psychological Trigger**: Appeals to people concerned about the social problem and what happens after treatment.

### The Insight

"These represent completely different psychological triggers. Ad 1 appeals to people interested in the business opportunity and financial model. Ad 2 appeals to people concerned about the social problem and what happens after treatment. Same business, completely different reasons to be interested. This is what true messaging diversification looks like - not different people saying the same thing, but different core reasons to care about your offer."

## Section: The Two Paths Forward

### Path 1: High Volume Approach (50+ Creatives Per Week)

**Description**: Producing fifty or more creatives per week

**Testimonial Source**: Public-facing Andromeda article from Meta; ecommerce brands went from 3-4 creatives per week to 50+ per week and maintained stability in results

**Messaging Requirement**: Do not necessarily need a wide range of diverse messaging

**Process Required**:
- Comfortable with relaunching campaigns weekly
- Follow ad fatigue protocols weekly
- Relaunch ad sets if using ad set budget optimization
- Complete overhaul of creative production process

**Resource Requirements**: Abnormal for most businesses; ~200 creatives per month needed

**Advantage**: Sheer volume can overcome algorithm changes

**Disadvantage**: Requires abnormal resources and systems

### Path 2: Diverse Messaging Approach (15-20 Unique Ads Per Month)

**Description**: Creating 15-20 completely unique ads per month with wide creative diversification

**Core Principle**: Each ad built around a different core reason someone would be interested in your offer

**Tested Results** (July 2025):
- No cost inflation
- Maintained consistent revenue
- Experienced by accounts that implemented this approach

**Key Difference from Path 1**: When you identify diverse reasons people want your product/service, you create entire unique ads around each reason rather than surface-level variations

**Advantage**: More manageable for most businesses; often produces better results with less resource investment
